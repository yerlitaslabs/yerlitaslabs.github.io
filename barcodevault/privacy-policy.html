1. Summary

BarcodeVault stores everything you record — counts, barcode values, quantities, locations, notes, the names of who counted, your product catalog, the codes you generate and backups — only on your device. If you turn on the optional Google Drive backup, copies of your backups are also stored in your own Google Drive, as described below. The developer does not receive this data, and the app has no user accounts and no analytics of its own. The app is free to download and shows ads from Google AdMob; the optional BarcodeVault Pro subscription removes them. For ads, for barcode recognition diagnostics and for Google Play services (purchases, app updates and rating requests), Google receives certain technical data from your device, as described in "Advertising and Google services" and "BarcodeVault Pro subscription". Information reaches the developer only if you decide to contact us yourself (see "Contacting us").

2. Data that stays on your device

Everything you create in the app — counts, barcode values, quantities, locations, notes, the names of who counted, your product catalog, the codes you generate, settings and backups — is stored only in the app's private storage on your device. The only exception is the optional Google Drive backup described below, which you turn on yourself. Camera images are analysed in real time in the device's memory to recognise barcodes; they are never saved, recorded or transmitted.

3. Advertising and Google services

The first three new counts each day start without an ad, and if no ad is available, the feature opens for that use anyway. While BarcodeVault Pro is active, no ads are requested or shown. Ads are provided by Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited for users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland; Google LLC elsewhere), which acts as an independent controller. To deliver ads, measure them, limit how often they appear and prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and sends to Google your device's advertising ID, your IP address (from which an approximate location may be derived), device and app information (such as model, operating system, language and app version) and information about the ads shown and your interaction with them. Where the law requires it — in the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and certain other regions — Google's consent form is shown and ads are personalised only with your consent; you can change your choice at any time in Settings › Ad privacy options. In Turkey, the app requests only non-personalised ads; this is determined from the country of your SIM card or mobile network, your time zone or your device region. Non-personalised ads are based on context (such as the app and the approximate location) rather than on your profile, although Google may still use the data above for frequency capping, measurement and fraud prevention. Elsewhere you can limit personalised ads by deleting or resetting your advertising ID in Android settings. Your counts, barcode values, notes, photos and documents are never passed to the ad SDK. Google ML Kit recognises barcodes on the device and does not send camera images or barcode contents; it may send Google technical diagnostics (device model, Android version, app version, a per-installation identifier, performance and error data). The barcode reader zxing-cpp and the code generator ZXing work entirely offline, and Android's automatic cloud backup is disabled for this app. Google may process this data in other countries, including the United States. How Google uses this data: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. The app is intended for adults and business use and is not directed at children. App updates and ratings: to tell you when a newer version is available and to download it when you tap "Update", the app uses Google Play's in-app update service; it occasionally asks you to rate the app with Google Play's in-app review service. Both run through the Google Play Store app on your device under Google's privacy policy and do not receive your records.

7. Photos and documents you scan

When you use "Scan image", the photo or PDF you select is read and analysed only in the device's memory to find codes. The file is not copied, uploaded or kept by the app, and the preview is discarded when you leave the screen. Codes that you choose to add to a count are stored like any other record. You can also send a photo or PDF to BarcodeVault from another app with Android's Share menu; the app then receives temporary access to that single file only and handles it in the same way. Files you open in the tools are read only on your device and are never sent by the app. A stock list you choose for comparison is kept in the app's private storage for that count, so that the scanner can show your progress, until you remove it in the tool or delete the count; it is not included in backups. A product catalog you import (barcodes, product names and optional product codes) is kept in the app's private storage until you delete it in the tool or uninstall the app, and it is included in the app's backups. When you merge counts, the files you add are only read on your device; the result is saved as a new count and the files themselves are not kept.

9. Backups and security

Backups are stored privately in the app on your device and are kept up to the number you choose. You can optionally encrypt backups with AES-256 using your own password; the password is protected by your device's secure key store and is never known to the developer. A forgotten password cannot be recovered by anyone. Protect your device with a screen lock and keep copies of important backups in a safe place. Optional Google Drive backup: if you connect your Google account in Settings, the app uploads a copy of each backup over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection to a hidden app folder in your own Google Drive and keeps there up to the number of backups you choose. The app requests only Google's drive.appdata permission, which gives it access to this folder and not to your other Drive files, and your email address, to show which account is connected. Google (Google Ireland Limited for users in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland; Google LLC elsewhere) stores these files under your Google account's terms and privacy policy, possibly in other countries including the United States; the developer has no access to them. If backup encryption is on, the files in Drive are also encrypted with your password. You can disconnect at any time in Settings (optionally deleting the copies in Drive), remove the app's access in your Google account settings (myaccount.google.com/permissions) or delete its hidden data in Google Drive's settings (Manage apps).
